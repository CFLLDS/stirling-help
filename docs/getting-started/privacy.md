# How people's privacy is protected

Stirling is a management view. It is designed so that you can run the organisation without seeing, or moving, personal information that you do not need.

## What Stirling does not show

| Area | What is kept out |
|---|---|
| Footcare | Counts only. No patient data leaves the clinical system |
| Home support | Clients appear only as a postcode area, never an address |
| Handyperson | Counts, minutes and money only. No customer, address or job note leaves that service |
| Card takings | Totals only. No cardholder data |
| Maps | Areas, not addresses. Visits are grouped into squares of roughly two kilometres, and any square with fewer than three visits is left out. No map tiles are loaded, so no location is sent to a mapping company to draw the picture |

## What Stirling does show

Three things on Stirling are personal data, and should be treated as such:

1. **Staff names on record-keeping breaches.** Carers are shown by first name and initial, because a manager needs to know who to speak to.
2. **The call-back list.** It shows the phone number of each person waiting, and sometimes a line about what they need, because somebody has to ring them.
3. **The attendance export.** The file you can download from Operations contains the time and location of each check-in and check-out.

## Your part

- Do not leave Stirling open on a screen that visitors can see.
- Do not take screenshots of the call-back list or the breaches panel into presentations. Use the Brief.
- Store the attendance export where you store other personal records, and delete it when you have finished with it.
- Sign out on a shared computer.
