# Find your way around

## The top bar

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Stirling** | The product name. It is on every page |
| The organisation list | Shows which organisation you are looking at. Most people have one. If you work across several, choose here |
| Your email address | Confirms who is signed in |

## The five tabs

Under the top bar are five tabs. The one you are on is underlined.

| Tab | In one line |
|---|---|
| **Brief** | One page, one decision |
| **Operations** | What is happening now, and what needs a person |
| **Telephony** | Calls, queues and who answered them |
| **Buildings** | Electricity, conditions and the network in your buildings |
| **Finance** | Cost, billing and what changed |

Every figure on the Brief also appears on one of the other tabs, where you can see it in more detail.

## How a page is laid out

Each page is built from panels. A panel has:

1. A **label** in small capitals, saying what is being measured.
2. A **large figure**, the answer.
3. A **sentence underneath**, giving the context you need to read the figure properly. Always read this line; it often says what is and is not counted.
4. A **source line** in small print, naming the system and the dates the figure came from.

## Sharing a page

The web address holds the tab and the reporting period. If you copy the address and send it to a colleague who has access, they will see the same tab for the same period.

## While a page loads

Panels show **Loading** for a few seconds while figures are fetched. Pages with a lot of history can take up to ten seconds.
