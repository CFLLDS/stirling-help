# Measured, estimated or written

Not every figure in Stirling is the same kind of figure. Stirling is careful to tell you which kind you are looking at, and it is worth learning to spot the difference.

## Measured

A count or a reading taken directly from a system: calls received, visits delivered, kilowatt hours used, money taken on the card readers.

You can rely on these as far as you can rely on the system they came from. The source line under the panel tells you which system that is.

## Estimated

A figure worked out from measured figures using assumptions, such as an hourly cost or a charge rate. Stirling marks these with **(est.)** in the label, or says so in the sentence underneath.

Examples:

- the value of unsold appointment slots
- the margin on a service
- staff time and cost avoided by the assistant

An estimate is only as good as its assumptions. Where Stirling uses them, it states them on the page, for example the hourly rate, the on-cost percentage and what has been left out. Read them before you quote the figure to anyone.

!!! warning "Estimates are not audited"
    Margin and savings figures are there to support a decision, not to go into the accounts. Stirling says "not audited" wherever this applies.

## Written

The **Brief** and the **briefing** at the foot of the Finance page are short pieces of text written by a language model from the figures on the page. They are labelled **Generated text, not a measurement**.

Treat them as a colleague's first draft. They are useful for seeing the shape of the period quickly, but:

- check any number in the text against the panel it came from
- treat any recommendation as a suggestion to discuss, not as advice
- do not paste the text into a board paper without reading it against the figures

## Empty is not zero

When a panel has no data, Stirling says so:

> No readings in this period. The panel is empty because nothing arrived, not because the value was zero.

This matters. A building that reports no electricity use has a meter or a connection problem; it has not used nothing. In the heat grids, the same idea appears as **no reading**.

## When two figures do not match

You will sometimes see two figures for what sounds like the same thing, for example appointments on one page and visits on another. Nearly always the reason is one of these:

| Reason | Example |
|---|---|
| They count different things | Appointments booked, against payments taken |
| They cover different dates | One panel looks back 28 days, another also looks forward |
| One is measured and one is estimated | Hours delivered, against hours rostered |
| They use a different base | A percentage of calls that reached the queue, not of all calls |

The sentence under each figure tells you what it counts. If two figures still cannot be reconciled, tell your LDS account manager.
